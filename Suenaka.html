The Tradition

About

Suenaka-kei Tetsugaku-hō Aikidō continues the study and practice of the aikidō transmitted by Roy Yukio Suenaka.

Rooted in Charleston, South Carolina, the tradition emphasizes practical martial effectiveness, disciplined training, and the philosophical principles of aikidō.

Its governing association, the Suenaka-kei Yūdanshakai, is being formed to bring together qualified practitioners and support continued transmission of the art.

The Lineage Founder

Roy Yukio Suenaka

Roy Yukio Suenaka was born in Honolulu, Hawaii. His father, Warren Kenji Suenaka, introduced him to budō and selected his early instructors. They included Henry Seishiro Okazaki, James Masayoshi Mitose, Yukiso Yamamoto, and kendō instructor Shūji Mikami.

Suenaka began aikidō during Kōichi Tōhei’s 1953 visit to Hawaii. His published biography records eight years of direct study with Morihei Ueshiba at Aikikai Hombu beginning in 1961 and attributes a menkyo kaiden certificate to Ueshiba that year.

His martial education included Okinawan karate and kobudō with Hohan Sōken and jūdō and jūjutsu at the Kōdōkan with Kazuo Itō.

Suenaka moved to Charleston in 1972. He served as Southeastern U.S. director for Tōhei’s International Ki Society until 1975, when he founded the American International Ki Development and Philosophical Society (AIKDPS). His book Complete Aikido: Aikido Kyohan appeared in 1998.

Suenaka-ha Tetsugaku-hō Wadōkai Aikidō is the historical name of his tradition. Suenaka-kei Tetsugaku-hō Aikidō is the name adopted for the present initiative in 2026.

Historical Record

Ranks & Awards

Suenaka’s published biography lists the following personal qualifications and awards.

  • 1961 · Menkyo kaiden in aikidō. A certificate of complete transmission attributed to Morihei Ueshiba.
  • 1977 · Hachidan in aikidō. Eighth dan. The biography leaves the awarding organization unspecified.
  • 1977 · Hachidan in karate and kobudō. Eighth dan in Matsumura Seito Hakutsuru Shorin-ryū.
  • 1972 · Rokudan in karate and kobudō. Sixth dan in the same tradition, attributed to Hohan Sōken.
  • 1970 · Sandan in Kōdōkan jūdō and jūjutsu. A dual third-degree certificate attributed to Kazuo Itō and Risei Kanō.
  • 1958 · Nidan in kendō. Second dan attributed to Shūji Mikami.
  • 1954–1958 · Hawaiian State Golden Gloves. Participation in boxing.

Instruction & Service

Teaching Experience

Suenaka.com records the following teaching appointments and programs.

  • Hawaii, 1956–1958. Assisted at Hawaii Aikikai after receiving his first black belt and before entering the U.S. Air Force.
  • Sacramento, 1958–1961. Opened what his biography describes as the first recognized aikidō school in the continental United States.
  • California and Nevada, 1958–1961. Taught the California Highway Patrol, Military Police at Mather Air Force Base, and the Strategic Air Command survival course at Stead Air Force Base.
  • Placerville and Encino. Taught young people and counselors at Boys Town in 1958–1960 and taught jūdō and aikidō at Encino High School in 1960.
  • Japan, Okinawa, and Vietnam, 1961–1972. Opened an Okinawa dōjō in 1961. His biography lists Military Police, Marines, Army Green Berets, Navy SEALs, the Air Commando program, and the Ryukyu Police Department among the groups he taught.
  • Charleston, 1973 onward. Opened an aikidō and karate dōjō in 1973. Taught Air Force Tactical Recovery Teams in 1973–1974 and Security Police in a program initiated by General Sadler.
  • Columbia, 1988–1989. Instructed police academy participants including State Police, FBI, SWAT, South Carolina Law Enforcement Division personnel, and correctional officers.
  • Costa Rica, 1991. Taught special task forces and Presidential Secret Service personnel through a Charleston City Police Department program.
  • Charleston, 1994. Taught a course for women and smaller-framed police officers.
  • Other students listed include jailers, bodyguards, State Department officials, Border Patrol personnel, bounty hunters, bail bondsmen, DEA personnel, U.S. Marshals, and Foreign Dignitary Protection Service agents.
  • Held recurring seminars in South Carolina, Georgia, Florida, North Carolina, Virginia, and New York, with additional seminars in California, Washington, and Colorado.
  • Authored Complete Aikido: Aikido Kyohan, published by Tuttle in 1998.

The Martial Art

Aikidō · 合気道

The Aikidō of Roy Suenaka

Suenaka’s aikidō drew on instruction from Morihei Ueshiba and Kōichi Tōhei alongside his broader martial education. Training emphasized effective self-defense, balance, coordinated movement, blending with attacks, throws, joint controls, and development of mind and body.

Equal attention was given to physical ability and personal development. The practice encouraged self-discipline, moderation, compassion, and respect for others.

Recognition & Certification

Rank & Transmission

Suenaka-kei distinguishes recognized aikidō rank from proficiency and teaching authority within its developing tradition.

External Rank Recognition

Aikikai Kyū / Dan

The intended pathway follows Aikikai grades. Dan recognition and registration with Hombu proceed through authorized Aikikai channels under the applicable practice, examination, recommendation, and approval requirements.

Kyū (級)
Student grades preceding black belt rank.
Dan (段)
Black belt grades beginning with first dan.

The Yūdanshakai does not independently award Aikikai dan grades.

Internal Proficiency & Transmission

Suenaka-kei

The Yūdanshakai is developing standards for documenting proficiency and granting teaching authority.

Mokuroku (目録)
A certificate or register documenting the material received and demonstrated.
Menkyo (免許)
A license granting permission to teach the material specified in the license.

Levels of Transmission

Shoden (初伝)
Initial transmission · Mokuroku / Menkyo
Chūden (中伝)
Intermediate transmission · Mokuroku / Menkyo
Okuden (奥伝)
Deep transmission · Mokuroku / Menkyo

Aikikai rank and Suenaka-kei credentials are separate qualifications. Neither automatically grants the other. A teaching license does not by itself authorize the holder to issue further credentials. This proposed framework does not represent historical licenses issued by Roy Suenaka.

Governance

The Yūdanshakai

末中系有段者会

Suenaka-kei Yūdanshakai
Suenaka-Lineage Dan-Ranked Practitioners Association

The Suenaka-kei Yūdanshakai is being established as the governing association of Suenaka-kei Tetsugaku-hō Aikidō. It is intended to unite qualified dan-ranked practitioners connected through Roy Suenaka’s teachings.

Its proposed responsibilities include maintaining the curriculum, evaluating proficiency, administering certificates and teaching licenses, and preserving continuity of instruction. Aikikai rank recognition remains with authorized Aikikai organizations.

Founding membership, examination standards, and formal governing authority are currently being established.

Continuity Across Generations

Historical Precedent

A martial tradition must be capable of continuing beyond the lifetime of the teacher who holds it together.

Approximately six years separate Morihei Ueshiba’s death from Suenaka’s independent founding. A comparable interval separates Suenaka’s death from the beginning of the present initiative.

From Ō-Sensei to Suenaka

1969
Morihei Ueshiba dies on April 26.
1974
Kōichi Tōhei leaves Aikikai.
1975
Roy Suenaka resigns from the International Ki Society and establishes AIKDPS.

From Suenaka to Suenaka-kei

2020
Roy Yukio Suenaka dies on February 8.
2020–2026
His students and their successors continue studying and teaching in different settings.
2026
Suenaka-kei Tetsugaku-hō Aikidō is named and formation of its Yūdanshakai begins.
Fidelity to the source must outlast allegiance to an intermediary.

Roy Suenaka’s example demonstrates that institutional independence and continuing fidelity to one’s teachers need not be opposites. Suenaka-kei seeks to carry this responsibility forward through documented proficiency, accountable teaching authority, and continuity of knowledge beyond changes in leadership.

The parallel concerns the interval between each teacher’s death and an independent founding. The circumstances of each transition belong to its own history.
